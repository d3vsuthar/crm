01 · Journeys

Lifecycle journeys across email, push and SMS

The rules and journeys that decide whether a customer hears from us, on which channel, and when we stop.

Problem
We sell in two markets over three channels, and the campaign calendar kept getting busier. It was easy for one customer to get an email, a push and an SMS on the same day, sometimes about something they'd already bought. We were sending more every month, and engagement wasn't keeping up.
My role
I designed the targeting and suppression rules, built the automated journeys, and wrote the tooling that applies those rules to every campaign we send.
Approach
Every send goes through the same checks in the same order. Has the customer opted in to this channel, and can we actually reach them? Are they in the right market? Did they just buy, or have they stopped opening? Are they already in another journey? On top of the campaign calendar, a set of always-on journeys reacts to what customers do on the site and app, and we test them against holdout groups to see what they really add.
Outcome
Every campaign follows one set of rules, and because the rules live in code, nobody has to remember them. More than ten always-on journeys run by themselves across the customer lifecycle, in English and Arabic, and each one can be measured the way section 03 describes.

Trigger

  • Cart or browse event
  • Purchase
  • Gone quiet
  • Calendar campaign

Eligibility

  • Opted in to the channel
  • Reachable device or address
  • Market (GCC / US)
  • Gender and brand fit

Hold back

  • Bought in the last few days
  • Rarely engages
  • Already in a higher-priority journey

Channel & timing

  • Email, push or SMS
  • English or Arabic
  • Send window per market

The always-on journeys

Each one starts when a customer does something, and stops as soon as they buy.

  • New customer

    Onboarding

    New app users get a short series introducing the brand's services and the categories they're likely to shop, by market and gender.

  • Browsing

    Product to bag

    Someone looks at a product but doesn't add it. They get a reminder, then a second nudge if they still haven't acted, tailored to language and to whether they have an account.

  • Considering

    Wishlist to bag

    Items saved to a wishlist but never added to the bag bring the customer back to them.

  • Ready to buy

    Abandoned bag

    Items left in the bag trigger an email and a push, run separately for the GCC and the US.

  • Brand building

    Awareness to first purchase

    For the retailer's own brands, customers move from an introduction to the brand to a nudge toward their first purchase.

  • Local moments

    Location and sale triggers

    Shoppers browsing in a city with fast delivery hear about it at that moment. During big sales, extra journeys move people from wishlist to bag and from bag to checkout.

03 · Measurement

Measuring incremental impact from CRM activities

Opens and clicks tell you someone saw a message. They don't tell you whether it changed what they bought. The question I wanted every journey and campaign to answer was simpler: would these customers have bought anyway?

The challenge
Our CRM platform reported engagement, not revenue. On top of that, email, push and SMS each took credit for the same orders using their own rules, so CRM looked bigger on paper than we could prove.
Control groups
Journeys and campaigns are tested against a random holdout group of customers who don't get the message, and they stay in that group for as long as the test runs. The difference in conversion and revenue between the two groups is what the message is really worth.
Experiment design
Before a test starts I write down what we expect to happen, the one metric that decides it, how long it runs, and what result would make us change course. Variants are split at random from the same audience and compared with the control group. Comparing with last month would mix in seasonality and sale periods.
Guardrails
Messages are tied to real orders with one set of rules and a fixed window for each channel, and each order is counted once. A daily health check compares delivery, opens, clicks, bounces and unsubscribes with the recent trend. If something breaks we know the same day, and we can tell a jump in send volume apart from customers actually losing interest.
What it changed
We now keep, change or switch off journeys based on what they add, rather than on clicks. One reminder journey that had run for a long time turned out to make no difference against its control group, so we moved that effort elsewhere. We also tightened attribution windows that were giving CRM too much credit. The numbers we report now hold up when finance and leadership look at them.
04 · Operations

Automating campaign production

Two markets, three channels and two languages add up to a lot of manual work for every campaign. I automated most of it. The team now spends its time on ideas and content, and we can run more campaigns without adding people.

The challenge
Each campaign used to be built by hand in the CRM tool, once per market, in English and in Arabic. Mistakes got through, like the wrong audience, a broken link or a product that had already sold out. Results were also tracked somewhere other than the plan.
What's automated
  • From plan to draft. The campaign calendar is the starting point. Email and push drafts are created in the CRM tool straight from it, with copy, layout, audience and schedule already filled in.
  • Building the email. Emails are put together from approved layouts, copy and product blocks, in both languages. Nobody hand-codes HTML.
  • Morning checks. Each morning, every upcoming campaign is checked for missing copy, translations, layouts, audiences and links. Featured products are checked against live stock.
  • Test sends. Proofs go to the right test list for each market and language.
  • Results. Performance flows back into the calendar twice a day, next to what was planned.
Built-in safety
The automation prepares and checks, and a person still approves and sends. Changes run as a dry run first, and the tooling has its own tests, so it's safe to keep building on.
What it changed
We run more campaigns a week with the same team, fewer mistakes reach customers, and getting from an idea to a send takes far less time. Every campaign goes through the same checks, whoever built it.
Next
I'm working on AI-assisted copy that drafts variations in the brand's tone of voice, based on past campaigns that did well. The aim is for every campaign to test its copy.
05 · Data · In brief

The data underneath

One customer view

Store and online purchases are joined into one record per customer, with spend split by channel. Our VIC and personal shopping teams work from it.

Data and consent in sync

Regular checks keep the website, the customer service system and the marketing platform in agreement. SMS opt-outs are synced across all of them, and incoming event data is monitored.

Toolkit

Python · SQL · BigQuery · MoEngage · Salesforce Service Cloud · Redash · NocoDB · scikit-learn · gensim · Flask · REST APIs

Figures come from live systems and are rounded. The retailer's name and its customer data stay confidential.
